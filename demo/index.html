Accessibility plugin for Embla Carousel 8.6 and 9

Accessible carousels everyone can use.

embla-a11y adds accessibility to Embla Carousel (“a11y” is short for accessibility). It keeps every visible slide readable, puts people in control of motion and never hides content anyone can see. It targets WCAG 2.2 AA.

Every demo on this page is a real carousel. Try them with the keyboard, VoiceOver, NVDA, TalkBack or voice control.

Why embla-a11y

  • Tested with real screen readers

    Every change runs VoiceOver, NVDA and Orca tests, plus automated tests in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit against Embla 8.6 and 9.

  • Built on research

    Based on WCAG 2.2, the ARIA Authoring Practices and what disabled people report about carousels. It aims at AA, plus the AAA criteria a carousel can meet.

  • Your language, your markup

    Every text comes from your page, in its language. Names and roles you wrote stay, and everything the plugin added goes away on destroy.

  • One plugin for Embla 8.6 and 9

    Works with the React, Vue and Solid wrappers and with server rendering.

What it does

  • Named region

    Screen reader users find the carousel by its visible heading.

  • What you see is what you reach

    Slides on screen, even partly, stay readable and reachable. Slides fully off-screen are skipped until they come into view.

  • Focus shows its slide

    Tab, screen readers and voice control never focus something you can't see.

  • Buttons keep focus

    At the ends, Previous and Next become unavailable without dropping focus.

  • Dots with names

    Every dot has a name, and the current one is marked.

  • Short announcements

    After a move, screen readers hear “Slides 4 to 6 of 10”, and nothing while it moves by itself.

  • Focus never gets lost

    When focused content disappears, focus moves to something nearby.

  • You control motion

    Autoplay pauses under the mouse, stops for good on interaction, has a stop button and respects reduced motion.

Demos

Use Tab, the buttons, a screen reader or your voice. There are no arrow key shortcuts, on purpose: screen readers use the arrow keys to read.

Testing with assistive technology

  • VoiceOver on a Mac: turn it on with Command+F5. VO means Control+Option. VO+U opens the rotor with headings and landmarks.
  • VoiceOver on iPhone or iPad: swipe right to move through a carousel, double-tap to press.
  • NVDA: press H for headings, D for landmarks.
  • Voice Control: say “Click Next” or “Click Save Vienna”. Every button is named by the text you see.

  • Basics

    Start here

      Previous and next buttons, dots and position announcements, set up as in the quick start.

      Try it

      • Press Next until the end. Next becomes unavailable, and focus stays on it.
      • With a screen reader you hear the new position after each press, for example “Slides 3 to 4 of 10”.
      • Open the landmarks list (VO+U, or D in NVDA): the carousel is a region named “Basics”.
      • The current dot is marked as current, and every dot says which slides it shows.
      • Slides fully off-screen are skipped. VoiceOver still says “list, 10 items”; NVDA counts only the slides on screen, for example “list, 2 items”.

      Guide: previous and next buttons

    • Partly visible slides

      Focus

        The next slide peeks in. It is not hidden from anyone: you can see it, so you can reach it.

        Try it

        • Press Tab through the Save buttons. When focus reaches the half-visible slide, the carousel moves until that slide is fully visible.
        • With VoiceOver, move through the slides with VO+Right Arrow: every slide is read, also the ones off-screen. When VoiceOver reaches a Save button, its slide comes into view.
        • Click a half-visible slide with the mouse: it does not jump away, so your click lands where you pressed.
        • Press Next three times, then move backwards past Previous with Shift+Tab or VO+Left Arrow: you land on the last slide you can see, not slide 10.

        Guide: focus brings its slide into view

      • Scroll by view

        Navigation

          Each press moves a full view, like turning a page. The dots and the announcements count pages instead of slides, with {page} and {pages} in the templates.

          Try it

          • Move to the dots with a screen reader: they say “Page 1 of 5”, “Page 2 of 5” and so on.
          • Press a dot and you hear the new page, for example “Page 3 of 5”.
          • Make the window narrower: with one slide per view there are ten pages, and the dot names follow.

          Guide: dot buttons

        • Arrow keys for the dots

          dotKeys: true

            Use the arrow keys to choose a slide.

            Ten dots would be ten Tab stops. With dotKeys: true, only the current dot is one, and the arrow keys move between the dots.

            Try it

            • Tab to the dots: you land on the current dot. One more Tab leaves the dots.
            • Left Arrow and Right Arrow choose the previous or next slide, Home and End the first and last. At the ends they wrap around.
            • The dots stay plain buttons, not tabs. A screen reader in reading mode keeps its arrow keys; they only move the dots while a dot has focus.

            Guide: fewer Tab stops for many dots

          • Loop

            Navigation

              The carousel has no ends, so neither button is ever unavailable.

              Try it

              • Press Previous right away: the carousel wraps around to Athens and Vienna, and you hear “Slides 10 and 1 of 10”, not “10 to 1”.
              • Press Next ten times: after Athens comes Vienna again, and you are back where you started.

              Guide: previous and next buttons

            • Autoplay

              Motion

                Moves on every four seconds. The stop button comes before the slides, so you reach it first.

                Try it

                • Tab to the rotation button: focus on the button alone does not stop the rotation. Press it to stop or start.
                • Move the mouse over the carousel: the rotation pauses, and the button still says “Stop slide rotation”. Move the mouse away, and it goes on.
                • Tab on into a slide, or press anywhere in the carousel: the rotation stops for good, and the button now says “Start slide rotation”.
                • Nothing is announced while the carousel moves by itself.
                • Turn on Reduce motion in your system settings and reload: the rotation does not start. The button can still start it.

                Guide: Autoplay, AutoScroll and the rotation button

              • AutoScroll

                Motion

                  Scrolls slowly and without a pause, like a ticker. The same rules apply as for Autoplay, including the pause while the mouse is over it.

                  Try it

                  • Press the rotation button to stop the scrolling, and again to start it.
                  • Tab into a slide: the scrolling stops for good, and the focused slide comes fully into view.
                  • Voice control works too: say “Click Save Paris”, and the carousel stops where it is.

                  Guide: Autoplay, AutoScroll and the rotation button

                • Fade

                  Effects

                    The slides lie on top of each other and fade instead of sliding.

                    Try it

                    • Tab into the slides and on: focus never stays on a slide that is faded out.
                    • Press Next with a screen reader and you hear “Slide 2 of 10: Berlin”: the announcement also says the slide's heading, with {heading} in the template.

                    Guide: focus brings its slide into view

                  • Removing slides

                    Focus

                      Each slide has a Remove button. Without the plugin, removing the focused slide would send focus back to the top of the page.

                      Try it

                      • Press “Remove Vienna”: focus moves into the next slide.
                      • Go to the last slide and remove it: focus moves to the slide before.
                      • Remove every city: the carousel itself takes focus.

                      Guide: focus never gets lost

                    • Skip past the every-slide demo

                      Every slide reachable

                      hideOffscreen: false

                        The same slides, but none is ever hidden. Every city shows up in find-in-page and in the heading and link lists. The price: the reading order runs through all ten slides, so reading backwards from the buttons jumps to slide 10. Use it only for carousels with a few slides.

                        Try it

                        • Press Tab before this demo: a skip link appears. It jumps past the carousel.
                        • Tab through all ten Save buttons: each slide comes into view in turn.
                        • Press Next three times, then Shift+Tab past Previous: you land on slide 10, and the carousel moves there.
                        • Search the page for “Athens”, or open the heading list: slide 10 is there. Both VoiceOver and NVDA say “list, 10 items”.

                        Guide: keeping every slide reachable

                      • Vertical

                        Layout

                          The same features on the vertical axis.

                          Try it

                          • Press Next: the slides move up, and you hear the new position.
                          • Press Next until the end: Next becomes unavailable and keeps focus.

                          Guide: focus brings its slide into view

                        • Städte in Europa

                          Deutsch

                            All texts come from the markup, here in German. The plugin never writes English of its own.

                            Try it

                            • Press “Weiter” with a screen reader: it switches to a German voice and says “Folien 3 bis 4 von 10”.
                            • Translate the page in your browser: the dot names are translated too, because they are text in the page.

                            Guide: position announcements

                          Drop it in

                          Register it like any other Embla plugin, last in the list. Pass the elements you have; every element you leave out turns its feature off.

                          Quick start with the full markup 

                          import EmblaCarousel from 'embla-carousel'
import emblaA11y from 'embla-a11y'

const emblaApi = EmblaCarousel(viewport, {}, [
  emblaA11y({ previousButton, nextButton, dots, announcer })
])

previousButton.addEventListener('click', () => emblaApi.goToPrev())
nextButton.addEventListener('click', () => emblaApi.goToNext())
